01 / ORIGIN

I’m a hematology and medical oncology physician. I trained at the NIH and the Cleveland Clinic and practiced across five cancer centers, academic, community, and hybrid. The workspace never changed: fragmented records, a dozen disconnected tools, and more than ninety clicks to order a routine therapy.

I became an EPIC-certified physician builder and joined clinical informatics to try to fix it from the inside. After enough walls, the lesson was simple. The system cannot be reformed from within. It has to be rebuilt.

So I started building. Local video. Open clinical models. A customized open-source EMR. All of it arranged around how a doctor actually thinks. Eight months later, a working clinic ran on a machine in my office.

02 / SPECIALTIES

Built for the specialties that broke first.

Much of subspecialty internal medicine is no longer a single high-stakes visit. It is follow-up over months: reviewing labs, judging response, adjusting therapy, writing the note, and watching what happens next. Those visits move online more cleanly than most of medicine, and they are the ones that benefit most from a system that can keep the record, the labs, and the thinking in one place.

We are starting with hematology and medical oncology. That is the field I practice, and it is where access is already strained: too few specialists, long waits for new patients, and long drives for people who do not live near a cancer center. The same design can move to other longitudinal specialties from there.

03 / ARCHITECTURE & STAGE

Real architecture. Honest stage.

On site by design. Open-weight clinical models run on a workstation-class GPU in the clinic. Inference, transcription, and record synthesis stay on that machine. No patient data leaves for a third-party network. That removes a large category of HIPAA exposure and the usual per-seat cloud cost.

The physician stays in the loop. Every output is presented for review and finalized by the physician, within the FDA’s Clinical Decision Support non-device safe harbor. The system assists. The doctor decides.

Where we are. A working end-to-end prototype exists today: video in the browser, real-time transcription with clinical entity extraction, local differential and note drafting, a customized OpenMRS record with two-way integration, and voice-driven order entry. We are pre-launch and hardening toward a first version in late 2026. It is being built by a practicing physician and an engineer who spent his career on large-scale data infrastructure in regulated environments.
