01 / ORIGIN

I’m a hematologist-oncologist. I trained at the NIH and the Cleveland Clinic and practiced across five cancer centers — academic, community, and hybrid. The one constant everywhere was the workspace: fragmented records, a dozen disconnected tools, ninety-plus clicks to order a routine therapy.

I became an EPIC-certified physician builder and joined clinical informatics to fix it from the inside, and after enough walls I learned the system can’t be reformed from within. It has to be rebuilt.

So I started building — a locally-hosted video stack, open clinical models, a customized open-source EMR, tied together around how a doctor actually thinks. Eight months later there was a working clinic on a machine in my office.

02 / SPECIALTIES

Built for the specialties that broke first.

Subspecialty internal medicine has quietly shifted toward longitudinal medication management and lab interpretation — review labs, assess response, adjust therapy, document, monitor. Those visits virtualize cleanly and benefit most from AI.

We ship hematology first, where the access gap is most severe — patients wait four to six months and drive hours for routine work-ups — and where our clinical depth is greatest.

The platform is modular by design: the same system extends to rheumatology, endocrinology, cardiology, and beyond as we add specialty modules.

03 / ARCHITECTURE & STAGE

Real architecture, honest stage.

On-prem by design. Open-weight clinical models run on a workstation-class GPU in the clinic via a local inference engine. Inference, transcription, and record synthesis all happen on the machine. No patient data traverses a third-party network — which removes a whole category of HIPAA exposure and per-seat cloud cost.

Physician in the loop. Every output is presented for review and finalized by the physician, operating within the FDA’s Clinical Decision Support non-device safe harbor. The system assists; the doctor decides.

Where we are. A working end-to-end prototype exists today — in-browser video, real-time transcription with clinical entity extraction, local model differential and note drafting, a customized OpenMRS EMR with two-way integration, and voice-driven order entry. We’re pre-launch and hardening toward a v1 in late 2026. Built by a practicing physician and an engineer who built large-scale data infrastructure in regulated environments.
